7-Day Morocco Itinerary

Marrakech, the Atlas Mountains, Aït Benhaddou, the Sahara Desert and Fes — the classic week in Morocco, with day-by-day routes and local tips.

← All Itineraries

Day 1: Arrive Marrakech. Settle into your riad, evening walk through Jemaa el-Fnaa. Day 2: Marrakech medina — Koutoubia, Bahia Palace, souks, rooftop dinner. Day 3: Atlas day trip — Ourika Valley, Amazigh villages, Setti Fatma waterfalls. Day 4: Depart for the Sahara via Aït Benhaddou and the Dades Valley. Day 5: Sahara Desert — camel trek, Erg Chebbi dunes, overnight desert camp. Day 6: Sunrise over the dunes, drive toward Fes via the Ziz Valley. Day 7: Arrive Fes, medina exploration, departure.

Who is this itinerary for?

This 7-day itinerary suits first-time visitors who want to see Morocco's highlights in one week — cities, mountains, desert and culture. It works for couples, families and small groups. The pace is moderate with 3–5 hours of driving on transfer days.

Day-by-day breakdown

Day 1 — Arrive Marrakech: Arrive at Marrakech Menara Airport, transfer to your riad. Evening at Jemaa el-Fnaa — food stalls, storytellers, musicians. Overnight in Marrakech.

Day 2 — Marrakech: Morning: Koutoubia Mosque, Bahia Palace, Saadian Tombs. Afternoon: souks — Semmarine, dyers' quarter, metalworkers. Evening: rooftop dinner with Atlas views. Overnight in Marrakech.

Day 3 — Atlas Mountains: Day trip to the Ourika Valley (1 hour from Marrakech). Amazigh villages, Setti Fatma waterfall hike, riverside lunch. Return to Marrakech. Overnight in Marrakech.

Day 4 — To the Sahara: Depart Marrakech via the Tizi n'Tichka pass (2,260m). Stop at Aït Benhaddou (UNESCO ksar). Continue through Ouarzazate to the Dades Valley. Overnight in Dades.

Day 5 — Sahara Desert: Morning: drive to Merzouga via the Ziz Valley and Erfoud. Afternoon: camel trek into Erg Chebbi dunes. Evening: dinner under the stars at a desert camp. Overnight in the Sahara.

Day 6 — To Fes: Sunrise over the dunes. Drive north via the Ziz Valley, Midelt and the cedar forests near Azrou. Evening arrival in Fes. Overnight in Fes.

Day 7 — Fes: Morning: medina orientation — tanneries, Bou Inania Madrasa, Al-Qarawiyyin. Afternoon: souk shopping, departure.

How much driving is involved?

Total driving for the 7-day itinerary: approximately 1,100–1,300 km. Transfer days (Day 4 and Day 6) involve 6–8 hours of driving with stops. Day 3 (Atlas) is a round trip of approximately 120 km. The itinerary is designed so you are never driving more than 8 hours in a single day.

When is the best time for this itinerary?

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer the best temperatures for this itinerary — mild in the cities, comfortable in the Atlas and warm but not extreme in the desert. Summer is hot in the Sahara but manageable. Winter brings snow to the Atlas passes and cold nights in the desert.

Local advice from Abrid

Start early on transfer days — mountain roads are winding and you want to reach your destination before dark. Bring layers for the Atlas and desert. Book the desert camp in advance for summer and winter. The Dades Valley is best photographed in late afternoon. Fes medina is a maze — hire a guide for the first day.

Frequently asked questions

Is 7 days enough for Morocco?

Seven days covers the highlights — Marrakech, the Atlas, the Sahara and Fes. It is tight but doable. If you have 10 days, you can add Chefchaouen and the north, or slow the pace.

Can I do this itinerary in reverse?

Yes. Start in Fes, drive south to the Sahara, then the Atlas and Marrakech. Many travelers prefer this direction — you end in Marrakech, which has the most flight options.

Do I need a 4x4 vehicle?

No. The main roads are paved. A comfortable sedan or minivan is sufficient. A 4x4 is only needed if you plan to go off-road or visit very remote areas.

Is it safe to drive in Morocco?

Yes, with caution. Main roads are good. Mountain passes are winding. Avoid driving at night. Use a licensed driver or guide — this is what Abrid provides.

What should I pack for a 7-day Morocco itinerary?

Layers, comfortable walking shoes, sun protection, a warm jacket for desert nights and the Atlas, a refillable water bottle, and a power adapter (Morocco uses European-style plugs).
