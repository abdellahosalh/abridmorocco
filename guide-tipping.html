Tipping in Morocco: Who, When & How Much

The no-awkwardness guide — typical amounts for guides, drivers, restaurants and camps, and when to keep your wallet shut.

← All guides

Tipping in Morocco is customary but never obligatory — a thank-you for good service, not a tax. Amounts below are what locals and experienced travellers typically give; treat them as customs, not rules, and always tip for genuine service, not for being asked.

Guides and drivers

  • Private day guide: typically 100–200 MAD for the day, more for exceptional service.
  • Private driver (multi-day): typically 50–100 MAD per day, handed over at the end of the trip.
  • Local site guides (tanneries, kasbahs, gorges): typically 20–50 MAD for a short visit.
  • Camel guides and camp staff: a shared tip of 50–100 MAD per group, left with the camp chief or guide.

Restaurants, cafés and hotels

  • Restaurants: 5–10% if no service charge is included — check the bill first.
  • Cafés: round up or leave 2–5 MAD.
  • Hotel porters and parking guards: 5–10 MAD, the small coins of everyday courtesy.

On Abrid Morocco trips

Tips are never included in our quotes and never expected — but if your driver or guide made the trip, the amounts above are exactly right, given directly to the person at the end. For multi-day journeys, one envelope at farewell beats daily handouts.

When not to tip

  • Don't tip for unsolicited "help" — directions you didn't ask for, photos you didn't request.
  • Don't tip officials at checkpoints; politeness is the currency there.
  • Don't feel pressured in tourist shops; a purchase is already the support.

Cash is king for tips

  • Break big notes early — markets and taxis run on small dirhams.
  • Keep a tip pocket — 5, 10 and 20 MAD notes separated, ready to hand over.
  • ATMs dispense large notes; shops and cafés break them willingly with a purchase.
